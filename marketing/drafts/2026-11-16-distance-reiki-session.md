You have heard friends talk about Reiki. Perhaps you live in Mumbai, Toronto or London, and the practitioner you would like to work with lives in Germany. Then someone mentions *distance Reiki*, and a fair question comes up: how can that work, and what would I actually do during the session?

This post walks you through a distance Reiki session step by step: how to prepare, what happens during the half hour, what people often notice, and what Reiki can and cannot do.

## First, what Reiki is

Reiki is a Japanese practice developed by Mikao Usui in the early twentieth century. The word joins *rei*, often translated as "universal", and *ki*, "life energy", the same idea that yoga calls *prana*.

In a session, the practitioner rests their attention, and in person their hands, on different areas of the body, one after another. It is gentle and quiet. Nothing is pressed, massaged or adjusted.

In distance Reiki, the practitioner works with focused intention instead of touch. Practitioners describe it as connecting with the person rather than with a place. You do not need to believe anything for the session to go ahead. You only need to be willing to lie down and rest.

## Before the session: a little preparation

- **Agree a time.** You and the practitioner choose a time that works across your two time zones. At Niramay, Richa gets in touch after you book to arrange it.
- **Find a quiet space.** A bed, a sofa or a mat on the floor is fine. Silence your phone and let the people you live with know you need half an hour undisturbed.
- **Wear something comfortable.** Loose clothes, socks if your feet get cold, and a light blanket nearby.
- **Set a simple intention, if you like.** A few words are enough: "rest", "letting go of this week", "clarity". You can also come with no intention at all.
- **Drink a glass of water** and avoid a heavy meal just before.

## During the session: what you do

Very little. That is the point.

1. Lie down on your back, or sit if that is more comfortable.
2. Close your eyes and take a few slow breaths. Breathing out a little longer than you breathe in (for example in for 4, out for 6) helps many people settle.
3. Let your breath find its own rhythm. You do not need to visualise, meditate "correctly" or stay awake.
4. If thoughts come, let them pass, like clouds. Return to the feeling of your body resting on the bed or the floor.

A distance session at Niramay lasts about 30 minutes. Afterwards, stay lying down for a few minutes before you get up.

## What people often notice

Everyone's experience is different, and there is no right or wrong one. People commonly describe:

- **Warmth or gentle tingling** in some parts of the body.
- **Deep rest.** Many people drift between waking and sleep, and some fall asleep completely.
- **Feelings rising to the surface.** Sometimes a memory or a few tears come up, and then pass.
- **Nothing in particular,** apart from having had half an hour of quiet. That is a perfectly good session too.

If something feels uncomfortable at any point, you can simply open your eyes and sit up.

## After the session

- Drink some water and give yourself a few unhurried minutes.
- If you like, write down anything you noticed: sensations, images, a mood. It can be interesting to look back on after a few sessions.
- Many people choose a lighter evening: a walk, an early night, less screen time.
- If you wish, share your experience with your practitioner. It helps them understand how you like to work.

## What Reiki can and cannot do

Let us be honest about this. A 2015 Cochrane review of Reiki for anxiety and depression found only three small studies and concluded that there is **not enough evidence** to say whether Reiki helps or not. Research on distance Reiki is thinner still.

So we do not present Reiki as a treatment for any illness, and it does not replace medical or psychological care. Please do not stop or change any medication or therapy because of a Reiki session. If you are dealing with a health condition, including your mental health, talk to your doctor or therapist first.

What many people do value is simpler: a protected half hour to lie still, breathe slowly and feel cared for. In a busy life, that alone can be worth a lot.
