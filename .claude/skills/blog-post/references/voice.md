# Niramay house voice

Taken from the published posts by Riju ("Stress, Immunity and Sleep: How Yoga Restores Your Balance", "You Always Have a Choice: Pleasure Now, or What Is Right for Life?") and Richa ("Beyond the Physical: A Deeper Understanding of Yoga", "Make Your Faith Bigger Than Your Fear"). If a new post sets a better example, update this file.

## The feel

A warm, knowledgeable teacher talking to one person. The tone is calm, never preachy and never salesy. Ancient wisdom is explained in everyday language and tied to the reader's daily life. Be kind to the reader: "you are not weak or disorganised", "not through guilt, but through gentle, steady practice".

## Openings

Start with something the reader recognises, in the second person:
- A scene: "You get a cold every time a deadline looms. You lie awake at 2 a.m. replaying the day."
- An image: "Imagine you are standing at a fork in the road."
- A question (Richa): "Have you ever wondered who the human being really is beyond this physical body that we see?"

Then one short paragraph saying what the post will cover.

## Structure

- Short paragraphs of 1–4 sentences, in plain words.
- `##` headings, short and concrete, often in the form "Label: what it means" ("The problem: a loop, not a list").
- Bold labels in lists: "**Pranayama (breath practices):** the direct handle on the nervous system."
- Sanskrit terms in italics with a plain gloss on first use: "*preyas*, what is pleasant, and *shreyas*, what is truly good". Name the source: Patanjali's Yoga Sutras, the Katha Upanishad, the S-VYASA tradition.
- Something to do: a concrete practice with numbers ("breathe in for 4 and out for 6 to 8, for 3 to 5 minutes"), sometimes as a small table or a "small practice for this week".
- Health topics include an honest **"What yoga can and cannot do"** passage: no cure, no replacement for medical care, when to see a doctor, and which conditions need adapted practice.
- Hedge evidence honestly: "consistent but modest benefits", "early studies are encouraging, although they are still small". Consistency matters more than intensity.

## Endings

Choose from:
- A one-line bold takeaway: "**Train the mind. Strengthen the intellect. Choose what is right, at the right time.**"
- The blessing: "*Sarve santu niramayah.* May all be free from illness, in body and mind."
- A gentle italic invitation, never a hard sell: "*At Niramay, we teach yoga as a practical, evidence-aware path to wellbeing, grounded in the S-VYASA tradition. If you would like to begin with guidance, we would love to practise with you.*"

## Excerpt

One to three sentences that open the topic and the promise of the post (its *content*, not a health outcome). It can be a short three-part line, like "Train the mind. Strengthen the intellect. Choose what is right, at the right time."

## Language notes

- **English:** British spelling (practise as a verb, practice as a noun, disorganised, fulfilment). Prefer commas and full stops to dashes.
- **German:** use **"du"** throughout the body. Published posts use "du", but one ("Stress, Immunsystem und Schlaf") switches between "du" and "Sie" halfway through. Don't repeat that: the check script flags any mix. The standard disclaimer that /write adds under a post uses "Sie". That's expected, and you don't write it yourself.
- **Translations:** carry over the meaning and warmth, not the word order. Keep Sanskrit terms and glosses. Translate headings so they read naturally, not literally.

## Avoid

- Hype, urgency and superlatives ("life-changing", "miracle", "instantly").
- Fear, guilt or shame as motivation.
- Talking down to other traditions, teachers, medicine or the reader.
- Long runs of theory with no link to daily life.
